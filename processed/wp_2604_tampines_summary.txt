**Summary of the Workers' Party (WP) Rally at Tampines GRC (Singapore Election 2025):**

The Workers’ Party held a rally in Tampines GRC for the 2025 General Election, introducing the team contesting in the GRC: Eileen Chong Pei Shan, Michael Thng Quan Wei, Muhamad Faisal Abdul Manap, Ong Lue Ping, and Tan Khim Tech, as well as various WP candidates for East Coast GRC, Aljunied GRC, and other constituencies. The rally was attended by residents, supporters, and voters from around Singapore.

**Key Themes and Points Raised:**

---

### **1. Cost of Living, Housing, and Family Concerns**
- Multiple speakers highlighted the **rising cost of living, housing affordability, and low fertility rates**, arguing these are key burdens for Singaporean families and young couples.
- WP proposes **essential goods (like basic food items) be exempt from GST**, and for BTO flats to be priced according to the median income of new buyers, including considering shorter leases (70 years) to lower costs.
- Concerns were raised regarding **resale HDB prices** and the issue of lease decay, with WP calling for more details about government housing plans.

### **2. Importance of Checks and Balances**
- Numerous candidates stressed the need for a **stronger opposition in parliament** as a check and balance to the PAP to ensure diverse views and better policies.
- Syvlia Lim and others illustrated how WP presence had already resulted in policy changes and pushed necessary debates, encouraging voters to "give your voice weight" in parliament.

### **3. Education and Mental Health**
- WP called for **smaller class sizes, mental health support in schools, postponing high-stress exams (like PSLE) to reduce anxiety among students**, and reforms to the education system to better support children and families.
- The need for greater mental health awareness and support, including the regulation of mental health professionals, was highlighted, alongside proposals for more compassionate social policies.

### **4. Support for Vulnerable and Marginalized Groups**
- WP candidates advocated for **improved job prospects for ex-offenders** (aligning with the Yellow Ribbon Project), and support for low-income groups and caregivers. 
- The party promotes **family care leave for caregivers of seniors** (analogous to childcare leave) and more flexible use of Medisave by seniors for chronic conditions.

### **5. Political Competition and the Role of NTUC**
- WP criticized the dominance of PAP and NTUC (National Trades Union Congress), pointing out the “safe harbor” for PAP candidates in NTUC and questioned the effectiveness of NTUC MPs in representing workers.
- The party called for a more accountable system, where MPs are not drawn from the same circles and where parliament enables real debate rather than being a rubber stamp.

### **6. Personal Stories and Sacrifice to Serve**
- Many candidates shared **personal reasons for stepping up**—from experiences as parents to disillusionment with current policies—and some even resigned from stable jobs to contest the election, signaling their commitment.

### **7. WP’s Manifesto and Track Record**
- The party has presented a "**122-page manifesto**" with **125 policy proposals** plus a list of policies previously introduced by WP and later adopted by the PAP government.
- They contrasted their manifesto with the PAP’s, criticizing the latter for being full of “motherhood statements” but lacking specifics.

### **8. Key Policy Proposals Mentioned**
- **Cost of living:** Exempt essential foods from GST; government buyback scheme for aging HDB flats; HDB to re-acquire coffeeshops to stabilize food prices.
- **Education:** Smaller class sizes; through-train programs to reduce exam stress.
- **Elderly:** Remove the retirement age; allow seniors to use Medisave more flexibly; introduce family care leave.
- **Mental health:** Stricter regulation of professionals; more awareness and support.
- **Support for families:** Childcare leave per child until age 12; reforms to reduce child-raising costs.
- **Parliamentary representation:** More non-PAP MPs for balanced debate.

### **9. Rally Spirit and Call to Action**
- The rally reinforced WP as a "loyal opposition" **loyal to Singapore and Singaporeans**, not any external party.
- WP candidates encouraged voters not to be swayed by small handouts (e.g., $2 breakfast deals, CDC vouchers), warning against transactional politics and urging long-term thinking.
- Voters were urged to vote for WP to create a more **inclusive, just, and fair Singapore**, emphasizing that *their vote is not just for themselves, but for their children and future generations*.

---

### **Conclusion & Closing Remarks**
The rally concluded with the Leader of the Opposition (Pritam Singh) summarizing WP’s commitment to Singapore and calling for voters to support a more robust and balanced political system. He made it clear that the party’s focus is a symbiotic relationship with Singaporeans—not with foreign elements or special interests—and repeated the call to *“Majulah Singapura”* (Onward Singapore), urging everyone to vote for WP for a more representative parliament and better Singapore for all.

---

**Main message:**
The Workers’ Party promises practical, people-centered policies to address cost of living, housing, education, mental health, and support for vulnerable Singaporeans. The party stresses the need for more opposition voices in Parliament to foster real debate, check the ruling party, and deliver a fairer, more balanced future for Singapore. They ask voters to look beyond short-term handouts, support substantive policy proposals, and vote WP for themselves and the next generation.
