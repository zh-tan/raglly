**Summary of Worker's Party (WP) Final Rally 2025 – Hougang SMC & Aljunied GRC**

**Context:**  
This is the transcript of the final Workers' Party rally for Singapore’s General Election 2025, held at Hougang SMC and Aljunied GRC. Key candidates include Dennis Tan Lip Fong (Aljunied GRC / Hougang SMC), as well as other prominent WP leaders such as Pritam Singh, Sylvia Lim, Jamus Lim, Gerald Giam, Faizal Manap, and new candidates.

**Key Themes and Messages:**

1. **Hope vs. Fear:**
   - WP repeatedly frames the PAP’s campaign as one based on fear (fear of instability, fear that estates will become slums, fear of being labeled as “free riders”), while the WP stands for hope, courage, and belief in Singaporeans.

2. **The Importance of Opposition:**
   - WP insists that a strong, rational, and responsible opposition is critical for checks and balances, policy scrutiny, and voicing out alternative ideas. They counter the narrative that opposition will "weaken" the government by stating PAP will still have a clear, even overwhelming parliamentary majority.
   - Examples are cited where WP proposals (unemployment support, flexible work, minimum wage, higher NS pay, tighter scam protection) eventually became mainstream policy.

3. **Parliamentary Voice & Accountability:**
   - Repeated assurance that votes are secret; voting for opposition is not only safe but productive, as it pressures PAP to work harder.  
   - Emphasis on WP MPs’ independence—not bound by the government whip—allowing them to question and debate policies substantively.

4. **Track Record in Municipal Management:**
   - WP candidates (esp. in Aljunied, Hougang, Sengkang) emphasized their decades-long experience running town councils, highlighting that their constituencies are not "slums", but well-maintained, with solid financial records and high ratings.  
   - Claims of “buy one get two/three free” – WP MPs working alongside PAP grassroots advisers and the People’s Association, providing active competition to benefit residents.

5. **National Issues – Cost of Living & Innovation:**
   - Strong focus on rising cost of living (housing, food, transport), lack of innovation and local enterprise growth, jobs and economic security.
   - Criticism of the PAP’s risk-aversion, slow innovation, and reliance on status-quo policies such as handouts.
   - Calls for a robust safety net, including unemployment support, retrenchment benefits, minimum wage, protection from discrimination, and strong support for caregivers.

6. **The Party’s Diversity, Renewal & Vision:**
   - WP introduces new candidates with diverse backgrounds, professional achievements, and a commitment to serve.
   - Assurance that the party is always renewing itself for the next generation.

7. **Addressing PAP’s Main Arguments:**
   - WP contests PAP’s claim that opposition weakens international standing or hinders crisis management, pointing out that even if WP wins every seat they contest, PAP will have a supermajority.
   - Questions the PAP’s talent claims, and the logic behind risking key ministers in opposition-contested wards.
   - Dismisses the “free pass” argument, noting that PAP “parachutes” candidates into GRCs with little prior work.

8. **History & Pledge:**
   - Veterans like Sylvia Lim and Pritam Singh remind voters of WP’s journey, historical moments (notably Aljunied takeover in 2011), and stress voting from conviction, not fear.
   - Rally ends with the citizens’ pledge, calling for democracy, justice, and equality.

**Conclusion/Call to Action:**
- WP urges Singaporeans to vote with hope and courage, for a better-balanced parliament, a more accountable government, and a brighter future.  
- Reminders about vote secrecy, and encouragement to spread the message to undecided voters.

---

**In Short:**  
**The Workers’ Party’s final 2025 rally called on Singaporeans to reject politics of fear, trust in their own courage, and vote for a future where checks and balances, constructive alternatives, and honest, accountable leadership are foundational. WP positions itself as a credible, proven, and responsible opposition, with a track record in both parliamentary debate and running towns, promising to work always for the people, not for self-advancement.**
