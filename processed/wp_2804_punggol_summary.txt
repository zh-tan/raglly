**Singapore General Election 2025 – Workers’ Party (WP) Punggol GRC Rally Summary**

**Context:**  
This is a transcript of a Workers’ Party (WP) rally at Punggol GRC for Singapore’s 2025 General Election. The WP team for Punggol GRC consists of Jackson Au Chee Meng, Alexis Dang Pei Yuan, Harpreet Singh Nehal, and Siti Alia Binte Abdul Rahim Mattar. The rally also featured key WP leaders and candidates from neighboring constituencies, addressing a maximum capacity crowd.

**Key Themes and Messages:**

**1. Cost of Living and Economic Hardship**
- Multiple speakers highlighted soaring living costs, from public transport and food prices to housing, healthcare, and childcare.
- WP criticized the ruling People’s Action Party (PAP) for raising the GST during a period of high inflation, which they say worsened the economic pain for ordinary Singaporeans.
- Proposals included exempting essential items from GST, implementing tiered utility rates, and establishing a national minimum wage of $1,600 for all full-time workers.

**2. Housing and Homeownership**
- Concerns raised about long BTO (Build-to-Order) flat wait times (now 4-5 years), making it difficult for young families to start homes; WP promised to push for shorter BTO wait times.
- The party wants the eligibility age for singles to buy BTO flats lowered to 28.
- WP criticized the government’s land pricing formula and public housing policy, arguing it fuels speculation and high prices.

**3. Support for Families, Caregivers, and Women**
- WP candidates related personal stories about family planning challenges due to costs.
- They advocated for direct preschool vouchers, and the extension of childcare subsidies to all families, including stay-at-home mothers.
- WP pledged programs for caregiver reintegration into the workforce.
- Emphasis was placed on women’s roles in opposition and politics, highlighting the slate of female WP candidates.

**4. Transport and Wages**
- WP pledged to establish a National Transport Corporation to reinvest profits into improved, more affordable public transport.
- National Service (NS) pay was deemed insufficient; WP called for NS “allowance” to be recognized as a proper salary with CPF contributions.

**5. Political Accountability, Diversity, and Transparency**
- Multiple speakers criticized “behind closed doors” decision-making, and what they see as a political culture relying on personal connections.
- WP promised open townhalls and direct engagement to set local priorities if elected in Punggol.
- They strongly disapproved of last-minute high-profile PAP “parachuting” (specifically DPM Gan Kim Yong to Punggol), arguing this shows PAP insecurity and lack of meaningful local representation.
- WP asserted that essential constituency developments (like the Punggol Digital District) are national plans that won’t be cancelled if opposition wins.

**6. The Role and Value of Opposition**
- The WP argued that a strong opposition holds the government accountable, citing examples where government policies eventually followed their proposals (unemployment insurance, NS pay, public defender’s office).
- They challenged voters not to fear "instability," noting the PAP will remain the government, with opposition representation bringing better debate, policies, and protection against “groupthink.”

**7. The Importance of This Election**
- WP leaders warned that electoral boundaries change often; current opportunities to elect opposition MPs may not repeat.
- Voting WP in Punggol would “save” ministerial salaries (by replacing three officeholders with WP MPs), and shift national politics toward more balance and transparency.
- The WP presented its candidates as relatable, diverse, and personally committed to serving residents.

**8. Call to Action**
- WP urged voters not to be swayed by fearmongering or loyalty to strongman politics, and to vote for change.
- They pressed the need for fresh perspectives, new ideas, and policies aimed at the real welfare of ordinary Singaporeans, not just GDP growth or elite interests.
- The final appeal: A stronger WP in Parliament will lead to a more just, innovative, and responsive Singapore.

**Notable Quotes and Slogans:**
- “Working for Singapore.”
- “Your vote is secret, your voice is powerful.”
- “Don’t settle for policies playing catch-up—demand solutions that address your life today.”
- “Say no to high-profile parachuting and politics of personal connections.”
- “Vote for change. Vote for hope. Vote for the Workers Party.”

**Summary Table of Main Proposals:**

| Issue/Problem                      | WP Proposal/Position                        |
|-------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| Rising cost of living               | Exempt essential items from GST, national minimum wage, structural solutions over handouts |
| Housing                             | Shorten BTO wait time, allow singles (28+) to buy BTO, reform pricing |
| Childcare/Family                    | Direct preschool vouchers, expand subsidies, return-to-work support for caregivers |
| Public transport                    | National Transport Corporation, invest profits in affordability/services |
| NS pay                              | Treat as salary with CPF, raise allowances  |
| Retirement                          | Advocate for inflation-adjusted CPF payouts |
| Political system                    | More diverse, balanced Parliament, end “connections-based” politics, fair allocation of funds |
| Opposition in Parliament            | Holds government accountable, introduces workable policies, blocks bad decisions |

---

**In conclusion:**  
The WP’s rally in Punggol GRC focused on Singaporean anxieties about costs of living, housing, and the need for a more inclusive, transparent, and accountable political system. They offered concrete policy alternatives, called out what they see as missteps and arrogance by the PAP, and passionately urged voters to seize the chance to send a new, credible, diverse opposition team to Parliament—insisting that it is necessary for Singapore’s democracy and social health.
