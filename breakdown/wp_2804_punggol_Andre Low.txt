### Summary of Andre Low’s Speech (WP Rally, Punggol GRC, GE2025)

**Introduction & Personal Connection**
- Andre Low greeted the crowd warmly, noting he lives nearby, though he didn’t grow up in Punggol, and joked about whether he “qualifies” as a son of Punggol.
- He emphasized that, while he may have been a stranger last week, he hopes now to be seen as someone who understands residents’ concerns, shares their hopes, and is ready to fight for the community.
- He highlighted his extensive ground engagement over the past two years, listening to residents.

**Key Issues Raised**
- **Rising Cost of Living:**  
  - Andre stressed that “everything is so expensive now,” a sentiment echoed by retirees, working adults, and young families.
  - He criticized government statements that inflation is falling, pointing out that, on the ground, prices for daily necessities are not dropping.
  - He argued that the issue is about dignity—whether Singaporeans can afford a decent standard of living in their own country.

- **GST Hike:**  
  - The Workers’ Party opposed the GST increase, not because they are against taxes, but because GST disproportionately affects lower- and middle-income households.
  - The 2% GST hike may seem small to those in “ivory towers,” but for many, it’s the difference between comfort and anxiety.
  - WP proposes exempting essential items (basic food, utilities, medicines, textbooks, school supplies) from GST.
  - He also called for tiered utility pricing, so those who use less (like seniors and small households) pay lower rates, rather than paying the same as those in luxury homes.

- **Housing Affordability:**  
  - Housing is the biggest expense for most Singaporeans.
  - He criticized the long BTO wait times (now 4-5 years, compared to 2-3 years in the past), saying the government’s new “goal” of under 3 years should be the maximum, not a target.
  - WP will push to restore shorter BTO wait times.
  - He advocated for allowing singles aged 28 and above (not just 35+) to apply for BTO flats, including single mothers, arguing that policies should reflect changing times.

- **Economic Vision & Minimum Wage:**  
  - Andre contrasted the PAP’s “trickle-down prosperity” model with WP’s vision of an economy that works for everyone.
  - He noted that while GDP grows, many Singaporeans feel left behind.
  - WP proposes a national minimum wage of $1,600/month, to ensure all full-time workers earn a living wage.

**Commitment & Call to Action**
- Andre pledged to work relentlessly for these policies in Parliament if elected, to be the residents’ voice on issues affecting their lives.
- He made clear he has nothing personal against his PAP opponent, but criticized the PAP for allowing these issues to fester—raising GST during a cost-of-living crisis and failing to keep housing affordable.
- He urged voters to choose change and hope on May 3rd, rather than “more of the same.”
- He ended by calling on voters to “send a strong message” to the government: vote for change, vote for hope, vote for the Workers’ Party.

---

**In summary:**  
Andre Low’s speech focused on the unaffordability of daily life in Singapore, the regressive impact of the GST hike, the need for fairer utility and housing policies, and the importance of a minimum wage. He positioned the Workers’ Party as the party with fresh ideas and a commitment to ordinary Singaporeans, and called on voters to make their voices heard by supporting WP for a more affordable and caring Singapore.
