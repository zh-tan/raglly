### **Summary of Harpreet Singh Nehal’s Speech (Punggol GRC, WP Rally, GE2025)**

**1. Addressing Residents’ Concerns:**
- Harpreet began by acknowledging three big questions that Punggol GRC residents have asked the WP team during their walkabouts:
    1. **What will you do for us if we vote for you?**
    2. **Is there a risk in losing DPM Gan Kim Yong if we vote WP?**
    3. **Will there be instability if we vote for the opposition?**

**2. What Will WP Do for Punggol Residents?**
- **National-level plans (e.g., Punggol Digital District)** will continue regardless of which party is elected; these are not dependent on the GRC’s MPs.
- The key difference is **how the constituency and Town Council are run**. WP promises to run the Town Council effectively, as they have done in Sengkang, and to listen to residents’ needs through town halls and other platforms.
- Harpreet pledged to **devote almost all his time to serving Punggol residents** if elected, reducing his legal work to focus on constituency and national issues.
- He pointed out that if WP is elected, **Singapore would save three ministerial salaries** (since the PAP team has three office holders), amounting to $15 million over five years. He humorously suggested that some of these savings should be returned to Punggol for local improvements.

**3. On the PAP’s “Parachuting” of DPM Gan Kim Yong:**
- Harpreet questioned why the PAP had to bring in DPM Gan at the last minute if they were confident in their record in Punggol.
- He criticized the idea that infrastructure funding depends on “good friends” in government, emphasizing that **public funds should be allocated fairly** to all Singaporeans, regardless of political connections.

**4. On the “Risk” of Losing DPM Gan:**
- Harpreet said he personally likes DPM Gan but noted that Gan is 65 and had previously been reluctant to take on the DPM role, suggesting he reflects the past, not the future.
- He argued that even if DPM Gan is not in Parliament, he can still contribute to Singapore in other ways.
- The election is about **the future direction of Singapore**, not about keeping senior office holders in a particular GRC.

**5. On the Direction of the PAP:**
- Harpreet criticized recent statements by PM Lawrence Wong warning of wealth taxes if more opposition MPs are elected, saying this shows the PAP is more concerned about the wealthy than ordinary Singaporeans.
- He argued that **inequality is a real threat** and that the WP will speak up for ordinary people.

**6. On Accountability and the AIA-NTUC Income Deal:**
- Harpreet raised the issue of the controversial AIA-NTUC Income deal, noting that DPM Gan, as chairman of MAS, had a role in approving it.
- He called on DPM Gan to answer questions raised by Tanuja Chair (former NTUC Enterprise CEO) about the deal, emphasizing that **real leadership welcomes scrutiny and accountability**.

**7. Will Voting WP Cause Instability?**
- Harpreet assured voters that the PAP will still form the government and Lawrence Wong will be PM, supported by the civil service and President Tharman.
- He said a strong, loyal opposition **makes Singapore stronger**, not weaker, by providing checks and balances.

**8. The Power of the Vote:**
- Harpreet urged Punggol residents to use their vote to send a strong message to the government, as the PAP will be watching both their vote share and the number of opposition seats.
- He emphasized that **voting for WP is voting for the future of Singapore**, not just for Punggol.

**9. Closing:**
- Harpreet concluded by reminding residents that their vote is significant not just for the present, but for future generations.
- He called on voters not to miss this opportunity to shape history and to **vote for the Workers’ Party**.

Harpreet Singh Nehal’s speech focused on reassuring Punggol residents that national projects would continue regardless of which party wins, promising effective and resident-driven local governance, highlighting the cost savings of electing WP, questioning the PAP’s motives and priorities, demanding accountability, and emphasizing that a strong opposition is essential for Singapore’s future. He urged voters to seize this historic opportunity to send a message for change by voting for the Workers’ Party.
