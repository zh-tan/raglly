### Summary of Jamus Jerome Lim’s Speech

**Jamus Jerome Lim** addressed the crowd as “280,000 bosses from Sengkang, Punggol, and Jalan Kayu SMC,” acknowledging the tough times Singaporeans have faced in recent years due to the rising cost of living. He highlighted how everyday expenses—food, transport, utilities, housing—have all become more expensive, and questioned what the PAP government has done to help Singaporeans cope.

He criticized the PAP’s approach of giving out more vouchers and handouts (CDC, GST, SG60, etc.), arguing that these are short-term, piecemeal solutions that only address symptoms, not the root causes. He said Singaporeans would rather be empowered to lift themselves up than to feel dependent on government handouts, which should be reserved for emergencies like COVID-19, not as a permanent feature.

Jamus pointed out that some government policies have actually made things worse, such as:
- Raising GST in 2023 while inflation was already high, despite other possible revenue sources.
- Restricting vehicle quotas, which pushed COE prices to record highs.
- Sticking to a land pricing formula that inflates HDB prices, making public housing less affordable and fueling speculation.

He argued that these are **structural, “homemade” problems** created by PAP policies, not just the result of global inflation. He explained how the government’s approach to HDB housing—balancing affordability, retirement adequacy, and home ownership—has become unsustainable, leading to a cycle of ever-increasing grants and subsidies that encourage speculation and drive up prices further.

Jamus also criticized the government’s reluctance to address high business rents, which are passed on to consumers, and its passivity in ensuring that incomes rise in line with costs. He noted that wage guidelines have only targeted lower-wage workers, while the middle class and retirees also struggle with rising prices. He called for stronger leadership to ensure incomes rise and for CPF interest rates to be adjusted to protect retirees’ savings from being eroded by inflation.

He argued that decades of PAP supermajority have led to **policy complacency, intellectual stagnation, and unimaginative thinking**. He warned against blindly uniting behind the PAP, saying that a lack of diversity in Parliament would be disastrous for Singapore’s future. He accused the PAP of using the “politics of fear” to discourage voters from supporting the opposition.

Jamus emphasized the need for **fresh ideas and new perspectives** in Parliament, saying the Workers’ Party has consistently championed innovative policies—many of which the PAP later adopts, sometimes 10 or 20 years later. He urged voters to support the Workers’ Party as the voice that truly listens to and empathizes with Singaporeans, and that is willing to speak truth to power.

He concluded by calling for a more balanced Parliament, with more opposition voices to ensure the government cannot ignore the people’s concerns, and to push for the structural reforms Singapore needs.
