**Summary of Aza Suliman’s Speech at SDP Sembawang GRC Rally (Singapore Election 2025)**

Aza Suliman, a candidate for Sembawang GRC with the Singapore Democratic Party (SDP), delivered a heartfelt and passionate speech focusing on the theme of bravery and the struggles of ordinary Singaporeans. Here are the key points from his speech:

---

### 1. **Bravery in Everyday Singaporeans**
- Aza began by praising the “brave people” of Sembawang, likening them to those in Bukit Panjang (where he previously spoke).
- He emphasized that bravery isn’t always loud or showy—it’s found in everyday acts: mothers sacrificing for their children, hawkers keeping stalls open despite rising costs, students working part-time to help with bills, and people persevering despite an unfair system.

### 2. **Questioning the PAP’s Narrative**
- He challenged the ruling People’s Action Party (PAP)’s repeated assurances that “everything is fine” and that HDB flats are affordable, the economy is strong, and the future is secure.
- Aza asked the crowd if these claims matched their lived reality: Can their children buy homes easily? Is retirement secure? Can they afford to fall sick or grow old?

### 3. **Critique of Current Housing Policies**
- As a real estate agent, Aza highlighted the sharp rise in HDB resale prices, noting that in Sembawang, even the cheapest 3-room flats cost nearly $500,000.
- He pointed out that HDB resale prices have increased by 37% in four years, with some flats appreciating by 60-65% in just five years.
- He argued that these rising prices mainly benefit banks, HDB (through loans), and the government (via CPF and property taxes), not young buyers or retirees.

### 4. **SDP’s Housing Solutions**
- **Non-Open Market Flats:** Aza promoted SDP’s policy of “non-open market flats”—homes sold without land cost, to be returned to HDB upon sale, making them more affordable for those who want a home, not an investment.
- **Rent-to-Own Scheme:** He proposed a rent-to-own scheme for first-time buyers, where rent payments contribute to eventual ownership, helping those who face life changes (like divorce) soon after getting a flat.
- **Abolishing ABSD for Singaporeans:** For the “sandwich class,” he suggested scrapping the Additional Buyer’s Stamp Duty (ABSD) for Singaporeans buying a second property, arguing that current policies prevent people from thriving and only allow them to “survive.”

### 5. **Call for Change and Courage**
- Aza called on residents to be “brave people who choose change,” urging them to vote for SDP and light the path forward for Singapore.
- He encouraged voters to reject fear and embrace hope, emphasizing that every vote is equal and the contest will be tight.

### 6. **Closing Poem**
- He ended with a poem celebrating the strength and hope of ordinary people, urging everyone to “rise with courage” and “let brave hearts rise.”

---

**In summary:**  
Aza Suliman’s speech was a rallying call for ordinary Singaporeans to recognize their everyday bravery, question the status quo, and support SDP’s alternative policies—especially on housing—to create a fairer, more hopeful future. He urged voters to be courageous, vote for change, and help build a Singapore where everyone can thrive, not just survive.
