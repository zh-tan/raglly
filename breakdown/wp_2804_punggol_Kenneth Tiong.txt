### Summary of Kenneth Tiong’s Speech (WP Rally, Punggol GRC, GE2025)

**Kenneth Tiong** introduced himself as the WP candidate for Aljunied GRC and explained why he stands with the Workers’ Party. He emphasized that WP is made up of ordinary Singaporeans driven by ideals, not by the pursuit of titles or material gain. He highlighted that WP members dedicate their time and resources because they believe in the possibility of a better Singapore and in giving Singaporeans a real choice.

Kenneth outlined the WP’s guiding principles: being rational, respectable, and responsible (“the three Rs”), and added a fourth: **Ready**. He declared that the WP is ready to listen, serve, and fight for Singaporeans.

He gave three concrete examples of how WP has pushed for policies that benefit Singaporeans:

1. **Work/Unemployment Support**:  
   - WP called for unemployment insurance as far back as 2006, repeating it in manifestos and in Parliament.  
   - It took 17 years for the government to introduce temporary support, and only in 2025 did a scheme take shape.  
   - Kenneth argued that WP saw the need early and pushed for security for workers, while the government delayed.

2. **National Service (NS) Allowance**:  
   - WP MPs, like Gerald Giam, pushed for better NS allowances, resulting in increases in recent years.  
   - Kenneth said NS is a sacrifice that deserves fair compensation, not just token words.

3. **Justice and Legal Access**:  
   - WP MPs called for fair access to lawyers for all, regardless of income.  
   - The government responded by creating a Public Defender’s Office, making the system fairer.

Kenneth highlighted a pattern: WP listens to the people, researches, and proposes solutions—often years before they become mainstream or are adopted by the government. He argued that WP’s persistent advocacy leads to real progress for Singaporeans, even if it takes time for the government to act.

He also addressed criticisms that WP is “PAP lite” (too similar to the ruling party). Kenneth countered that it’s not about being left or right, but about focusing on the future and tackling today’s challenges. He joked that if voters want a preview of sensible PAP policies of 2030, they should vote WP in 2025 and get those policies earlier.

Kenneth criticized the government for taking too long to implement needed policies, saying that the cost of delay is borne by ordinary Singaporeans. He urged voters not to settle for delayed or watered-down solutions, but to vote for a party that is ready to act now.

He concluded by calling on voters to make their vote count, to vote for the Workers’ Party—a party that is always ready to work for Singapore and to deliver solutions that matter to people’s lives.
