**Summary of Suraya Akbar’s Speech at the SDP Sembawang GRC Rally (Singapore Election 2025):**

Suraya Akbar, one of the SDP candidates for Sembawang GRC, delivered a concise but focused speech at the rally. Here are the key points she made:

1. **Gratitude to Supporters and Volunteers:**
   - She warmly thanked the crowd for turning up despite the rain, and expressed deep appreciation to all SDP members, changemakers, and especially the volunteers who have worked tirelessly for the campaign.
   - She encouraged continued support through the purchase of SDP merchandise (like bears) and called for more people to sign up as polling agents, emphasizing the importance of their role in the election process.

2. **Clarification on Housing Policy:**
   - Suraya directly addressed recent remarks by Minister Ong Ye Kung, who claimed that SDP had copied another opposition party’s (PSP) housing proposal.
   - She firmly rebutted this, stating that the SDP’s Non-Open Market (NOM) housing scheme was first introduced in 2019, well before similar proposals by other parties.
   - She explained the NOM scheme: HDB flats would be sold based on construction and administrative costs only, without inflated land costs, making them more affordable for lower- and middle-income Singaporeans and stabilizing long-term housing affordability.
   - She criticized the accusation of imitation as factually incorrect and a distraction from real housing issues like runaway resale prices, housing insecurity for young families, and financial burdens on working citizens.
   - She challenged Minister Ong to address what happens to HDB flats at the end of their 99-year lease, a question often left unanswered.

3. **On Town Upgrading and Public Funds:**
   - Suraya highlighted that town council improvements, infrastructure upgrades, and estate enhancements are paid for with taxpayers’ money, not as gifts or rewards from any political party.
   - She condemned the politicization of public funds and insisted that such developments belong to all Singaporeans, regardless of how they vote.

4. **SDP’s Approach and Vision for Sembawang:**
   - She contrasted SDP’s approach with the PAP’s, criticizing the latter for treating Sembawang as an afterthought and only promising upgrades during election periods.
   - Suraya outlined SDP’s people-first town plans, emphasizing accessibility (such as lifts at overhead bridges), more seating for seniors, and respect for the elderly.
   - She proposed practical improvements: 
     - Elevators at major overhead bridges for accessibility and dignity.
     - More seating with armrests and back support in high-traffic areas for seniors.
     - Creation of public, collaborative co-working spaces for students, freelancers, and retirees, so people don’t have to buy expensive coffee just to have a place to study or work.
     - Establishment of a youth mental wellness center in Sembawang.
     - Environmental protection for Sembawang Park Beach, including regular water testing and enforcement against illegal dumping.
     - Support for stray cats through funding food, medical care, and microchipping, working with volunteers.

5. **Call for Change and Voter Empowerment:**
   - Suraya encouraged voters not to be swayed by fear-mongering or the idea that only the PAP can deliver improvements.
   - She urged residents to elect MPs who will truly represent and fight for them, not just during elections but every day.
   - She called for a move away from “turf wars” and back to focusing on policies that matter to people’s lives and futures.

6. **Closing Message:**
   - Suraya ended with a rallying call to vote for SDP for compassion, transparency, and a town plan that puts “heart back into the heartland.”
   - She reiterated SDP’s slogan: “Thrive, not just survive,” and wished for a better, more caring Sembawang and Singapore.

**In summary:**  
Suraya Akbar’s speech was a passionate defense of SDP’s original housing policy, a critique of the politicization of public funds and election promises, and a presentation of practical, people-centered plans for Sembawang. She called for voters to choose change, support SDP, and help build a more inclusive, caring, and accessible community.
