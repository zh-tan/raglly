### Summary of Michael Thng Quan Wei (Michael Tong Huang)'s Speech

**Michael Thng Quan Wei** introduced himself as a Workers’ Party candidate for Tampines GRC. He began by reminiscing about his first Workers’ Party rally experience in 2006 as a junior college student, describing how that event opened his eyes to the desire among Singaporeans for a more inclusive and stronger Singapore. He credited the rally-goers for inspiring his political journey and expressed hope that young attendees today might similarly be inspired to step up in the future.

Michael highlighted the Workers’ Party’s track record of making a real difference on important issues—not through slogans, but through substantive work. He emphasized that the party has consistently challenged the “false choices” presented by the PAP government, such as:
- The claim that supporting single mothers would undermine families,
- The idea that low-wage workers must not receive help to avoid a “handout mindset,” and
- The assertion that political diversity threatens stability.

He argued that the Workers’ Party has helped shift the national conversation, bringing previously marginal issues into the mainstream. For example, today, the debate is not whether retrenched workers need support, but what kind of support; not whether minimum wage is needed, but how broad it should be; and not whether single-parent families deserve equal treatment, but why they can’t be treated fully.

Michael noted that even the PAP now admits it does not have a monopoly on good ideas, questioning why then it should have a monopoly in Parliament.

He criticized the government for withholding information and for “gaslighting” the public—for example, insisting that GST hikes during high inflation are good policy, or that CDC vouchers are sufficient to address cost-of-living pressures. He also mocked the inefficiency of government projects, such as the new ERP system, and rejected the idea that more opposition would distract the PAP from serving Singaporeans.

Addressing Tampines residents directly, Michael said that many had asked why the Workers’ Party took so long to contest there. He assured them that the party is now present and ready to give residents a real voice in Parliament. He called on everyone to mobilize their friends and family to vote for the Workers’ Party, emphasizing that only collective action can bring about change.

He concluded by thanking the crowd for their support and urging them to vote for the Workers’ Party to build a better, more inclusive Singapore where all voices are heard.
