### Summary of Dr. Paul Anantharajah Tambyah’s Speech

Dr. Paul Tambyah began by expressing his support for Dr. Chi Soon Juan, describing him as a rare “conviction politician” in Singapore, in contrast to most others who are more pragmatic. He recounted Dr. Chi’s academic achievements, his early reputation as a neuropsychologist, and his decision to enter politics in 1992—a move that changed his career but, according to Dr. Paul, benefited Singaporeans.

Dr. Paul urged for a focus on real issues rather than personal attacks, and called for higher standards of political decorum.

He addressed the **minimum wage debate**, criticizing the PAP’s preference for complex solutions like the Progressive Wage Model over a straightforward living wage. Dr. Paul argued that a guaranteed living wage would ensure basic dignity for all workers, prevent exploitation, and provide fair competition for jobs. He cited the example of a bus company offering higher wages and attracting many Singaporean applicants, showing that Singaporeans are not “picky” but want fair pay.

The main focus of his speech was the **third pillar of the SDP manifesto: Respect for Our Planet**. Dr. Paul emphasized the reality and dangers of climate change, noting that 2024 was the hottest year on record in Singapore. He explained how rising temperatures increase health risks, such as mosquito-borne diseases.

Dr. Paul called for:
- Accelerating Singapore’s transition from fossil fuels to renewable energy.
- Stopping investments in coal-burning facilities, including those overseas.
- Making Singapore a regional leader in renewables, leveraging local technology and talent.
- Empowering local communities to develop and promote renewable energy, rather than relying solely on top-down government initiatives.
- Preserving green spaces and imposing a moratorium on forest clearing, including secondary forests, to protect the environment and mental health.
- Taking environmental impact assessments seriously.

He warned that relentless development and population growth have led to the destruction of forests and green spaces, which affects air quality and mental well-being. Dr. Paul stressed that Singaporeans must act as good stewards of the environment for future generations, and that the SDP’s approach is to ensure everyone can thrive, not just survive, in the face of climate crisis.

He concluded by urging voters to “vote for change, vote for the SDP,” and to protect Singapore’s future by supporting environmental sustainability and social justice.
